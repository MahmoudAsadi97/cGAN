# Fresh generation diagnostics

The frozen candidate checkpoints retain material distribution mismatches on these development profiles. Flow models approach the resampling/copula baselines on aggregate sliced Wasserstein distance, but their ramp and PV0-group behavior remain problematic. The cGAN variants remain underdispersed. This packet does not establish a publication-ready Phase 1 model.

The completed diagnostic run includes **all 12 declared candidate checkpoints, two source-only controls, and three baselines**, each evaluated with generation seeds **20261023 and 20261024**: 34 draws. The same **16,384 inner-validation profiles** were reused, with 256 profiles in each of 64 HP/EV/PV × day-type × season conditions. Checkpoints were already selected using the earlier fixed generation seed 20261022; these diagnostics performed no reselection. Fresh generation seeds measure sampling variation on the same meters and checkpoints. They provide neither new independent meters nor additional training-seed replication. The reported sampling SD uses only two generated draws; it is not a confidence interval or confirmation on untouched validation data. This remains post-test development inside the original TRAIN partition.

All cGAN candidates below use global TRAIN-fitted standardization, Adam beta1=0 and batch size 512. `d` denotes discriminator dropout; candidates 000–005 have no energy-distance auxiliary loss. Candidates 010–011 use dropout 0.4 and energy weight 1. Flow candidates use either an isotropic Gaussian source or a Gaussian source with TRAIN-fitted temporal correlation, learning rate 0.001, batch size 512, and 32 solver steps. Source-only controls apply the corresponding Gaussian source and physical inverse transform without a learned map or equipment conditioning; identical sources shared across training seeds appear once per source kind. Mean, resampling and copula baselines were fitted on the same full inner TRAIN.

The completed update budgets are **30,000** for the six ordinary cGAN and four flow candidates, versus **6,000** for the two energy-loss screening candidates. Their selected checkpoints can occur earlier, as shown below. Equal update counts also do not establish equal computation across cGAN and flow. Consequently the energy screen is not a matched-budget final comparison. Blank training fields in the CSV and dashes below mean “not applicable” for controls/directly fitted baselines, not a missing candidate outcome.

Values below are arithmetic means over the two fresh draws for each fixed checkpoint/control. SW1 and all error metrics are lower-is-better. Normalized SW1 divides physical-profile SW1 by the TRAIN range, 33.619001388549805 kWh. Ramp W1 compares signed adjacent-hour power differences without wrapping across midnight. Variance MAE compares hourly variances in kWh². “Variance ratio” is the macro average of each condition's generated-to-real mean hourly variance ratio, reported as `diversity_ratio`; 1 denotes matched variance for that metric, not complete distributional agreement. Each PV0 percentage averages the two draws' fraction of profiles with at least one negative hourly net-import value.

Ordinary cGAN checkpoints have mean normalized SW1 **0.00670–0.00703**, compared with resampling **0.00527** and copula **0.00551**. Their variance ratios are **0.636–0.689**, indicating insufficient within-condition spread on this selected cohort. Flow SW1 improves to **0.00535–0.00553**, and its variance MAE is lower, but variance ratios are **1.203–1.288**. The baselines also exceed 1 on this cohort, so these ratios are descriptive comparisons to the selected real profiles rather than calibrated model-failure thresholds. Flow ramp W1 remains **0.138–0.203 kW**, versus resampling **0.046 kW**. Correlated-source flow has higher ramp error than isotropic flow at both training seeds despite competitive SW1 and peak errors; temporal correlation in the source alone did not resolve generated ramp behavior. The untrained source controls are substantially worse on aggregate SW1 (**0.0209–0.0217**), showing that source structure alone does not account for the learned flows' distribution fit.

The shorter energy-loss screen has variance ratios **0.793–0.916**, nearer 1 than ordinary cGAN, but worse SW1 (**0.00810–0.00902**), ramp error (**0.167–0.173 kW**) and variance MAE (**0.810–0.823 kWh²**) at these checkpoints. This illustrates why one favorable aggregate ratio does not establish an improvement. Budget differences preclude a definitive method ranking, and both screening outcomes are retained.

The source-PV=0 subset contains **8,192 real profiles**. None has a negative hourly value in this selected real sample. Across individual generated draws, ordinary cGAN puts at least one negative hour in **35.97–47.00%** of PV0 profiles; flows in **73.90–80.27%**; and energy-loss cGAN in **62.34–69.43%**. The mean magnitude of negative hourly values, summed per day and averaged over every PV0 profile, is **0.0836–0.1427 kWh/day** for ordinary cGAN, **0.3715–0.5022** for flow, and **0.7818–1.0799** for energy-loss cGAN. Thus the discrepancy is not described only by a sign-count metric. Resampling and copula each average **0.00610%** PV0 days with a negative hour across the two draws (individual draws: 0% and 0.01221%), with mean magnitude about **0.00000238 kWh/day**. The conditional mean has zero such days. Source-only isotropic and correlated controls have approximately **100%** and **94.3–94.6%**, respectively, with around **8.0–8.2 kWh/day** of negative-slot magnitude.

This is a material mismatch with the observed source-PV=0 groups. The source flag is not independently verified equipment ground truth, so the result does not establish that every generated negative value is physically impossible. A day containing a negative hour is also different from a day whose total net energy is negative; both fractions are retained separately in the CSV. The report leaves generated values unchanged and reports the observed behavior. It does not establish a remedy or justify changing the values after evaluation.

All learned candidates and the mean/copula/source controls have zero full-row matches to inner TRAIN under the recorded little-endian float32 quantization test; resampling has a match fraction of 1 by construction. Zero quantized exact matches do not establish privacy, absence of near copies, or novelty.

`metrics.csv`, `summary.json`, and `manifest.json` are byte-identical copies of the completed artifact files. They preserve all draw outcomes, evaluation sources/hashes, checkpoint/configuration mapping, selection indices and generation seeds. `pv0_diagnostics.csv` contains one row per method/checkpoint and draw, with training seed, completed/selected updates, real and generated PV0 proportions, negative-slot magnitude, and the source JSON path/SHA-256. Proportions in the CSV are fractions, not percentages; magnitude averages over all PV0 days. `DERIVATION.json` binds every input, copied file, derivation script and derived CSV by SHA-256. The per-method source JSON files remain under `artifacts/phase1-development/fresh_diagnostics/`; their hashes are also bound by the copied evaluation manifest. To reproduce only this extraction/copy operation, run `python reports/phase1-development/fresh_diagnostics/derive_pv0.py` from the repository. The script reads diagnostic JSON/CSV artifacts only and does not load datasets/models, retrain, regenerate profiles, recompute evaluation metrics, reselect checkpoints, or clip outputs.
